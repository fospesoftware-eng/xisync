02 · Thesis
The one-line argument

Small exporters lose deals in the gaps between tools.

Between a WhatsApp message and a cleared container, the work still runs on spreadsheets, personal phones and agents. XiSync closes those gaps in one workspace.

01

Own the conversation

Buyers message on WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook. The team answers from WeChat. XiSync is built to pull every channel into one inbox and return the reply on the channel it arrived on.

02

Own the compliance graph

Every destination market demands a different set of mandatory fields per shipment. We are encoding each field, each document and each regulatory change — per country, not per template.

03

Own the money flow

The business is the entry ticket. The ad budget is the revenue: the model is to place global media directly and keep 10% of the spend we manage, with no agent in the middle.

What we are not

Not a marketplace — we do not compete with Alibaba or Made-in-China for buyers. Not a freight forwarder — we price and predict, carriers move. Not a generic CRM — the compliance and document layer is the reason a customer cannot simply rebuild this in a spreadsheet.

What has to be true

Someone pays $139 per export business per month, and enough of them spend on global media that the 10% compounds on top of the subscription. The rest of this deck is about proving those two things are reachable.

04 · Why now

Five shifts that make this buildable now, not in 2022.

Buyers

Buying moved to chat

Cross-border sourcing conversations now start in WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook — channels a WeChat-based team cannot natively answer.

→ A channel-native inbox is a prerequisite, not a feature.

Rules

Compliance fragmented

EU GPSR has applied since December 2024; CBAM moves into its definitive phase from 2026; tariff schedules keep changing. Per-shipment field matching no longer fits in a template.

→ Per-country rule data becomes an asset.

Cost

AI became affordable

At $4–10 of inference per business per month, a $139 subscription can carry real AI work on every document and every message — the margin simply did not exist before.

→ The unit economics finally close.

Sellers

Marketplace fatigue

Sellers want their own domain, their own buyer data and their own ad account. Paying rent to a marketplace for demand is no longer the only option they see.

→ Own-site plus own-ads is the pull.

Policy

Hainan Free Trade Port

Hainan is being built as a free-trade-port jurisdiction with island-wide customs operations, giving a China-facing service business a compliant, RMB-native operating base.

→ Fospe China can invoice locally.

The window

Enterprise trade software is priced for companies with a customs department. The SME segment is large, digitally active and has no native operating system — but it is only reachable while the channels are still open and the category is unformed.

18 · Business model

One subscription, one commission, one expanding budget.

Stream 1 · Subscription
$139 / business / month
From Year 1

Per export business, billed annually in advance. The anchor price is RMB 1,000 a month at 7.20 RMB/USD — $1,667 a business a year. Every person who touches the order — sales, documentation, logistics, finance, management — is included. No per-head pricing, no implementation fee.

Benchmark: $1,667 a year is roughly 7% of the fully loaded cost of one export salesperson in China — and it replaces the tooling of five.
Stream 2 · Media commission
10%of managed ad spend
Scales with the customer

We place ads directly on global platforms and keep 10% of the spend we manage. The customer's ad budget grows as their catalogue becomes advertisable — so revenue grows without a new business being sold.

Assumed: 35% of businesses buy media, at RMB 3,000 (≈$417) per buying business per month. That adds $175 a year per average business on top of the subscription.

Expansion, without a new product

One account covers the founder, the sales desk, documentation, logistics and finance. There is no per-head meter to argue about, so adoption spreads inside the business; expansion then shows up in ad budget and in a second exporting entity, not in a licence count.

Why not charge more

$139 a month is a deliberate ceiling: it sits below the level at which a founder needs a committee decision, and it is defensible against the agency and marketplace spend it replaces. Price is a wedge, not the margin story — the model takes 92.6% gross margin at this price.

Method

Revenue is recognised on the average of opening and closing paying businesses, which is deliberately conservative against a plan that grows every month. Subscription = average businesses × $1,667. Commission = average businesses × 35% buying × $417 × 12 × 10%, or $175 per average business.

Paying businesses are the plan, not reach. Exit ARR is capped under $10M — 5,300 businesses, $9.76M — so Year 5 is constrained by conversion and ARPU, not reach. Paying is 0.106% of the 5M market; registration is 2%.

Gross new businesses = net additions + churned businesses (churn on average businesses). Sales and marketing = gross new businesses × blended CAC and includes partner revenue share. The quarterly engine uses the same assumptions, so quarters sum to the annual line.

Not in the model

Expansion revenue from existing accounts (business growth and ad-budget growth are captured only through the business target), any financing income, any grant or subsidy, and any revenue from non-China customers. All of these would improve the numbers; none is claimed.
